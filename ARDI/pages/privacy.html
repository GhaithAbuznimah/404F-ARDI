3. Personal data we may collect

Depending on the features you choose to use, ARDI may process the following categories:

Account and contact information

  • Name and email address.
  • Authentication information, such as password hashes, account identifiers and security/session records. ARDI should never store or log your plaintext password.
  • National number only if the Operator establishes and explains a specific, necessary and lawful purpose for collecting it. It should not be collected merely because a form has a field for it.

Farm and agricultural information

  • Farm or field name, approximate or precise field location, boundaries or coordinates you provide.
  • Crop choices, planting plans, crop-rotation history, soil information, irrigation details and other agricultural information you submit.
  • Questions, preferences and information you enter into ARDI tools.

Technical and usage information

  • Device, browser, operating-system, connection and diagnostic information.
  • Security logs, timestamps, access events and error reports, configured to avoid recording passwords, national numbers or unnecessary personal data.
  • Cookie or similar technology information, if such technologies are enabled.

Consent and communication preferences

  • Whether you accepted the Terms and Conditions and acknowledged the Privacy Policy, including the relevant document versions and timestamp.
  • Your separate optional marketing preference, including when you change or withdraw it.

Data minimisation: We should only request information that is directly relevant and necessary for a defined ARDI purpose. Farm information may become personal data when it can be linked to an identifiable farmer or account holder.

4. Why we use data and the applicable legal grounds

ARDI may use personal data for clearly defined purposes, including:

  • Creating and administering accounts, authenticating users and providing requested workspace features.
  • Saving farm profiles, field information and crop-planning preferences.
  • Generating and explaining agricultural insights requested by users.
  • Maintaining service security, preventing misuse, troubleshooting and protecting data integrity.
  • Responding to support requests, privacy requests and complaints.
  • Meeting applicable legal obligations and establishing or defending legal claims where permitted.
  • Sending optional product news or promotional updates only where the user has separately opted in and applicable law permits it.

Under Jordan's Personal Data Protection Law No. 24 of 2023, prior consent is a central requirement for processing, subject to the specific exceptions and conditions set out in the Law. Before processing begins, the controller must provide information including the data, processing start date, purpose, duration, participating processors, security controls and profiling information where applicable. ARDI's Operator must document the legal ground for each processing activity and obtain valid, specific and explicit consent where required. A checkbox acknowledging this notice is not automatically a substitute for any distinct consent required by law.

5. Farm, field and location information

Farm and field locations are used to support location-specific environmental context, mapping and agricultural planning. Location information can identify or be linked to a person and may therefore be personal data.

Only provide location details you are authorised to share. The Operator should configure access controls so that a user can access only their own farm records and any records explicitly shared with them. Precise coordinates should not be made public by default.

9. How we protect information

The Operator must implement security, technical and organisational measures appropriate to the nature, scope and risks of processing. ARDI's intended safeguards include:

  • HTTPS/TLS for data in transit and encryption for sensitive stored information where appropriate.
  • Argon2id password hashing; plaintext passwords must never be stored.
  • Role-based access controls, least privilege and access reviews.
  • Secure secret management, separation of encryption keys from databases and restricted administrative access.
  • Data minimisation, secure backups, recovery testing, logging that avoids sensitive values, vulnerability management and security testing.
  • Incident response procedures and staff or team access confidentiality.

No internet service can guarantee absolute security. If a personal-data security incident occurs, the Operator will assess it and make notifications to the competent authority and affected individuals as required by applicable law.
